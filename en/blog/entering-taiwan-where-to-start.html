How Should Foreign Brands Start When Entering Taiwan?

By Yvonne Wang · Co-Founder · Growth Marketing Consultant
Published 27 November 2026

Foreign brands entering Taiwan should start by confirming that Taiwan is the right market for their product right now, then localise for how Taiwan actually communicates: Traditional Chinese written for Taiwanese readers, a platform mix led by LINE, and product labels that meet the Commodity Labeling Act. Test with a small budget, learn what resonates, and only then scale.

How do you know if Taiwan is the right first market?

Taiwan is the right first market when three conditions hold: there is proven demand for your category, your price stays competitive after import and logistics costs, and someone can make decisions quickly in Taiwan’s time zone (GMT+8).

Taiwan is compact and highly connected. DataReportal reports 22.4 million internet users in Taiwan in October 2026, or 97.3 percent of the population. That makes Taiwan a good place to test a digital-first launch, but a connected market is not automatically the right one for every product.

Before committing budget, answer three questions:

  1. Is there proven demand for your category in Taiwan, and which local brands already serve it?
  2. Can you price competitively once import, logistics and local margins are included?
  3. Do you have someone who can make decisions quickly in GMT+8?

If the answers are unclear, a short market assessment costs far less than a Taiwan launch that stalls.

Which social platforms matter most in Taiwan?

LINE, Facebook, YouTube and Instagram reach the largest share of Taiwan’s internet users, and Threads is the fastest-growing platform. In GWI survey data published by DataReportal (Q2 2026), these are the shares of internet users in Taiwan aged 16 and above who used each platform in the past month:

Three things stand out:

  • LINE is Taiwan’s default messaging app. Many brands use LINE Official Accounts to keep in touch with customers after the first purchase.
  • Facebook and YouTube still reach most of Taiwan. They remain the backbone of paid reach.
  • Threads has grown quickly in Taiwan. Meta’s advertising tools showed 8.95 million Threads users in Taiwan in late 2026, up from 6.65 million a year earlier.

Advertising reach tells a similar story. In late 2026, Google’s and Meta’s advertising tools showed YouTube reaching 19.1 million users in Taiwan, Facebook 17.1 million and Instagram 12.6 million. These are ad-reach figures rather than monthly active users, but they show how much of Taiwan each channel can reach.

A brand entering Taiwan does not need every platform on day one. Choose two or three that match where your buyers spend time and how they research your category, and do them well.

Why does localising for Taiwan mean more than translation?

Localising for Taiwan means writing Traditional Chinese the way Taiwanese consumers actually talk about your category, not converting copy written for another market. Taiwanese readers notice vocabulary and phrasing written for other Chinese-speaking markets, so copy that is technically correct can still feel foreign. Good localisation adapts tone, examples and product names, and checks what local competitors already say so your brand can stand apart.

Hong Kong and Taiwan should be treated as separate markets. Both use Traditional Chinese, but language habits, channels and consumer expectations differ, so messaging usually needs recalibrating for each.

What do Taiwan’s product labelling rules require?

Taiwan’s Commodity Labeling Act requires product labels to be written primarily in Chinese, with English or other languages allowed as a supplement (Article 11). For imported goods, the label must also include the name, address and service phone number of the local importer or distributor, and the foreign manufacturer’s name (Article 6).

Brands selling physical goods in Taiwan should plan labelling early. Specific product categories, such as food and cosmetics, can be subject to additional laws, so confirm the rules for your category with a qualified local adviser before shipping.

What order should a Taiwan launch follow?

A Taiwan launch works best in five steps: validate, localise, seed trust, test paid media, then scale.

  1. Validate: confirm demand, pricing and the competitive landscape in Taiwan.
  2. Localise: build your messaging, product pages and labels for Taiwan.
  3. Seed trust: work with creators (KOLs/KOCs) whose audiences match your buyers, and build word-of-mouth on LINE, Facebook, Instagram and Threads.
  4. Test paid media: run small Meta and Google campaigns to learn which messages and audiences convert.
  5. Scale what works: increase spend on proven combinations and set up a LINE Official Account for repeat communication.

Paid results usually move first, while word-of-mouth and organic reach take longer to build, so plan for both from the start.

Which creators should brands work with in Taiwan?

For considered purchases, brands entering Taiwan usually build more trust with knowledgeable creators than with the creators who have the largest following. For products people research before buying, such as software, electronics or premium goods, reach alone rarely persuades Taiwanese buyers.

When assessing a creator in Taiwan, check three things:

  • How closely the creator’s audience matches your buyers.
  • How the creator has explained similar products before.
  • Whether the comments show genuine discussion rather than empty engagement.

How should brands measure a Taiwan launch?

Measure a Taiwan launch on three time horizons, because each moves at a different speed:

HorizonWhat to watch
Short termCustomer acquisition cost, conversion rate and return on ad spend
Medium termSocial engagement and word-of-mouth reach
Long termGrowth in branded search volume and social share of voice

Set up tracking before the Taiwan launch. Without it, you cannot tell which messages, channels or creators are worth scaling.

What mistakes do foreign brands make when entering Taiwan?

The most common mistakes are treating Taiwan as an extension of another market and scaling before anything is proven:

  • Reusing copy written for another Chinese-speaking market. It saves time at first and costs trust later.
  • Launching on too many platforms at once. Thin activity everywhere is less effective than strong activity in a few places.
  • Scaling ad spend before the message is proven. More budget amplifies a weak message; it does not fix it.
  • Leaving labels until the goods are ready to ship. Relabelling late can delay a Taiwan launch.
  • Running Taiwan from too far away. Decisions that wait days for a reply lose momentum in a fast-moving market.

FAQ

Do imported products need Chinese labels in Taiwan?

Yes. Taiwan’s Commodity Labeling Act requires labels to be written primarily in Chinese, with English or other languages allowed as a supplement. Imported goods must also show the local importer’s or distributor’s name, address and service phone number, and the foreign manufacturer’s name.

What is the most used social platform in Taiwan?

LINE. In GWI survey data published by DataReportal for Q2 2026, 92.0% of internet users in Taiwan aged 16 and above said they used LINE in the past month, followed by Facebook at 79.3% and YouTube at 73.3%.

How long does it take to see results after entering Taiwan?

Eastbright Global typically sees advertising and conversion metrics start to move within one to two months, while word-of-mouth and organic traffic generally take three to six months to show a clear upward trend.
