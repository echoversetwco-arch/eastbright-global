AI makes marketing output cheap because a team can now produce ten creative versions in the time one used to take. Judgement becomes more valuable because the bottleneck moves from making content to deciding which content is on-brand, accurate and worth running. Teams that keep people at those decision points get more from AI than teams that simply produce more.

## How widely do organisations use AI now?

AI use is now mainstream across organisations, and marketing is where many see the revenue impact. McKinsey’s The State of AI 2026 survey, fielded from 4 May to 8 June 2026 with 1,719 participants in 97 countries, found:

| Finding | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Respondents reporting regular AI use in at least one business function | Nearly nine in ten |
| Respondents whose organisations use AI in three or more functions | 56%, up from 51% |
| Respondents attributing at least some EBIT impact to AI | 37%, about the same as the year before |
| Function most often credited with AI-driven revenue gains | Marketing and sales |

The gap between near-universal use and a 37% share reporting financial impact shows that access to AI tools is no longer the advantage. How teams use them is.

## Why does more output not mean better marketing?

More output does not mean better marketing because every extra version still needs someone to judge it. When a team can produce ten versions a day, the bottleneck shifts from production to selection: which message fits the brand, which claim is accurate and which version deserves budget. Without clear judgement, extra output adds review work, inconsistency and risk rather than results.

## Which marketing decisions should stay with people?

The decisions that should stay with people are the ones that carry brand, legal or budget risk. These include:

- **Brand fit:** whether a message sounds like the brand and suits the market.
- **Accuracy:** whether every product claim, figure and comparison is true and supportable.
- **Market judgement:** whether an angle will resonate with local buyers or misfire culturally.
- **Budget decisions:** which versions to fund, scale or stop.
- **Sensitive information:** anything involving customer data or confidential business information.

AI can draft, summarise, translate and generate variants for all of these. The final call belongs to someone accountable for the outcome.

## How can a marketing team keep judgement strong while using AI?

A marketing team keeps judgement strong by building the structure that tells people and AI what good looks like. Four foundations make the biggest difference:

1. **Map the workflow:** identify which steps AI should speed up and which decisions stay with people.
2. **Set review standards:** define what every piece must pass before it goes live, and who signs it off.
3. **Build a brand knowledge base:** collect brand voice, product facts and past work so AI output starts closer to on-brand.
4. **Train the team:** give people the skills to brief AI well and to judge its output with confidence.

## What does this mean for cross-border marketing?

For cross-border marketing, judgement matters even more because AI can produce fluent copy in a language the team does not speak natively. Fluent is not the same as right for the market. Local review of vocabulary, tone and cultural references remains essential, especially in markets that share a script but not a voice, such as Taiwan and Hong Kong.

## FAQ

### Will AI replace marketing teams?

The evidence points to AI changing marketing work rather than replacing it. As AI makes production cheaper, the most valuable work shifts towards deciding what to say, checking that it is accurate and choosing where to spend budget.

### Where should a marketing team start with AI?

A marketing team should start by mapping its current workflow to find the steps where AI adds the most value, then set review standards before scaling output. Starting with tools before standards tends to create more content without better results.

### How should a team review AI-generated marketing content?

A team should review AI-generated content against clear standards for brand fit, factual accuracy and market suitability, with a named person signing off before publication. Anything involving sensitive information should be handled by people.
